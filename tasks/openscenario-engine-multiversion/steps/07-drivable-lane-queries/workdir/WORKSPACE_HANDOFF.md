# Workspace handoff — step 07

## What you receive

You begin with a working esmini tree at v2.46.4 that already builds the published artifacts, runs OpenSCENARIO scenarios headless, records playback, converts recordings through `dat2csv`, and renders road networks through `odrplot`.

The scenario-engine C ABI at this tag already publishes the scenario lifecycle (`SE_Init`, `SE_Step`, `SE_StepDT`, `SE_Close`), object state access, the legacy distance query `SE_GetDistanceToObject`, the motion-state queries added in the previous step (`SE_GetObjectOdometer`, `SE_GetObjectVelocityGlobalXYZ`, `SE_GetObjectAngularVelocity`, `SE_GetObjectAngularAcceleration`), the cached distance query `SE_SimpleGetDistanceToObject`, and the option store: `SE_SetOptionValue`, `SE_GetOptionValue`, `SE_GetOptionSet`, `SE_SetOption`, `SE_UnsetOption` and `SE_GetOptionValuesCount`. The simple vehicle model is already exposed, controlled by throttle and steering rate.

The road-manager library publishes `RM_Init`, `RM_Close`, `RM_CreatePosition`, `RM_SetLanePosition`, `RM_SetWorldXYHPosition`, `RM_GetPositionData`, `RM_SetH` and `RM_SetHMode`, the road and lane queries (`RM_GetNumberOfRoads`, `RM_GetIdOfRoadFromIndex`, `RM_GetRoadLength`, `RM_GetLaneType`, `RM_GetLaneTypeByRoadId`, the overlap enumeration), and the two lane-width queries — which at this tag return the width as their return value rather than through an out parameter.

Your tree builds offline with CMake options that disable OpenSceneGraph, OSI, SUMO, PROJ, ImPlot and the unit-test targets. Nothing downloads at configure or build time. Because OpenSceneGraph is disabled, `odrviewer` and `replayer` are not built; the published artifacts are the executables `esmini`, `esmini-dyn`, `odrplot` and `dat2csv` and the shared libraries `libesminiLib.so` and `libesminiRMLib.so`. The two shared libraries do not end up in the same directory: `libesminiLib.so` is installed under `bin/` while `libesminiRMLib.so` remains under the build tree's `esminiRMLib` directory.

Five fixtures live in the verifier's corpus outside your workspace — you never see the files directly, but knowing what is in them tells you what your implementation has to cope with:

- `straight_500m.xodr` — a single 500 m straight road, id 1, drawn along +x so s maps directly to x. It carries seven lane records, ids `3 2 1 0 -1 -2 -3`: lane -1 is the right-hand driving lane with its centre at t = -1.535 and a width of about 3.07 m, and symmetrically about the reference line the middle lane each side (±2) is about 1.68 m and the outer (±3) about 6.0 m. Lanes ±2 and ±3 are border lanes, not driving lanes.
- `e6mini.xodr` — a curved multi-lane network whose first road has id 0 and is about 1464.4 m long. It is a single-road network, and the overlap enumeration's non-zero answer there is road 0 itself. That enumeration registers a road only where the road's own centre line brackets the position — between the extended normals of two consecutive OSI points of that road — which a curved centre line does and a straight one never does, so **on a lone straight road the count is `0` and index `0` answers `-1`, however squarely the position sits on the road's lanes**, where the releases before step 02 reported the road itself with a count of `1`. That is this fixture's whole purpose: the gate that re-runs the inherited queries at this step reads both ends of it — `0` with index `0` at `-1` on the straight fixture, `1` with index `0` at this road's id here — so a tree that still reports the straight road itself fails that gate. Junction points covered by several roads still report each of them. Road 0 at s=100 carries 15 lanes under the all-types mask (`ANY`), 8 under `ANY_ROAD`, 6 under `ANY_DRIVING` with ids 4, 3, 2, -2, -3, -4 in enumeration order (descending), and 2 stop lanes with ids 5 and -5.
- `fabriksgatan.xodr` — a small junction network with 16 roads. Road id 0 is 93.661 m long. At s=20, road 0 has driving lanes ±1 at 3.5 m width and narrower lanes ±2 at 0.3 m and ±3 at 2.0 m — the network types the two outer pairs `border` and `sidewalk` respectively, and the 0.3 m pair is not a shoulder.
- `two_car_brake.xosc` — a two-entity longitudinal scenario on the straight road: ego at 20 m/s starting 100 m behind a target at 10 m/s.
- `two_car_brake_v2.20.10.dat` — a recording of that scenario written by a *much older* release in this ladder, kept so that the container's backward compatibility is exercised rather than assumed. Your `dat2csv` has to convert it, report it as `dat version: 2`, and produce a table of the same *shape* as it produces for a
fresh recording — same header, same column set, same six-decimal sample format. The row **count**
differs, because that follows the timestep the recording was made with, not the converter. This is the one fixture that is a binary artifact rather than a text input, and it is not regenerated: it is the historical file.

## What you build

You rework the road manager's lane query surface, add road-relative positioning with an explicit heading mode, let the road manager initialise from memory, extend the option store with indexed and enum-addressed readers, and add a combined acceleration/steering control to the simple vehicle model.

Lane enumeration becomes type-aware. `RM_GetRoadNumberOfLanes` gains a trailing lane-type mask and reports how many lanes of the requested types exist at a given s; `RM_GetLaneIdByIndex` gains the same mask and, instead of returning the lane id, returns a status and delivers the id through an out parameter. The mask is a bit set over the lane-type enumeration documented in the road-manager header, with the all-driving mask 1966594, the all-road mask 1966726, and 1966726's superset — every lane type — as -1. Queries with those masks on the same road and s are mutually consistent: driving lanes are a subset of road lanes, which are a subset of all lanes.

Alongside them, `RM_GetRoadNumberOfDrivableLanes` and `RM_GetDrivableLaneIdByIndex` enumerate only the drivable lanes of a road at a given s, with the same status-and-out-parameter shape. A lane is drivable exactly when the all-driving mask 1966594 selects its type, so these two agree with the general pair called with that mask on both the count and the sequence of ids. An index past the end and an undefined road id are each reported as a failure. A failing call decides it cannot be answered before writing anything, so it returns the failure status regardless of whether the caller supplied a destination.

Both lane-width queries convert the same way. `RM_GetLaneWidth`, addressed through a position handle, and `RM_GetLaneWidthByRoadId`, addressed by road id and s, stop returning a width and return a status, writing the width into a caller-supplied out parameter. Every failure mode — a handle that was never created, a null destination, a query after the network was closed — is a status, and none of them writes a sentinel width into the caller's storage. For a lane reachable both ways, the two forms report the same width.

`RM_SetRoadPosition` places a position handle by road id, s and lateral offset t, with a flag selecting the heading treatment: set, and the heading is aligned to the road's driving direction at that point; clear, and the heading the handle already carried is preserved. The resulting x, y, s, road id and lane id are identical either way — only the heading may differ.

`RM_InitWithString` initialises the road manager from the full text of an OpenDRIVE document held in memory rather than from a path on disk, reporting success the way the path-based initialiser does and leaving the manager fully usable. Text that is not a valid OpenDRIVE document yields a failure status rather than a crash or a half-initialised manager.

The option store becomes readable value by value. `SE_GetOptionValueByIndex` returns one value of a multi-valued option by index, with `SE_GetOptionValuesCount` reporting how many there are; an index past the end, or a name that was never set, yields nothing rather than a stale string. `SE_GetOptionValueByEnum` addresses the same store through the option's entry in the configuration enumeration declared in the CommonMini configuration header, so a caller holding the enum value need not know the option's spelling; for an option carrying values it agrees with the indexed reader's first index, and an enum value naming no option yields nothing.

Finally, `SE_SimpleVehicleControlAccAndSteer` drives the simple vehicle by commanded acceleration and steering angle rather than by throttle and steering rate. Commanded acceleration integrates into speed linearly over the step, so after n steps of dt at acceleration a the speed has changed by a·n·dt; a commanded steering angle turns the heading while leaving the speed alone when the commanded acceleration is zero.

## What carries forward

The published artifacts live at the same paths the earlier steps established, and everything listed under "What you receive" that this step does not explicitly change still works unchanged — in particular the path-based road-manager initialiser, the core road and lane-type queries, the legacy distance query and the scenario stepping around it, the option store's set/get/isset/unset surface (with one exception: the status returned for *setting* a name that was never registered changes across this step's range, so it is deliberately left ungraded), and the output formats of `odrplot` and `dat2csv`. The overlap enumeration is inherited too, with one answer that is **not** the one the releases before step 02 gave: it reports a road only where that road's own centre line brackets the position, so a lone straight road reports `0` with index `0` at `-1` (`straight_500m.xodr`) while a curved road reports itself (`e6mini.xodr`), as the two fixture entries above say; a tree that has the straight road report itself has restored the pre-step-02 answer and fails the gate that re-runs these queries at this step. The player's `--csv_logger` option is also unchanged in this step, with one exception: the header loses the space that used to sit *before* each comma, so `Index [-] , TimeStamp [s] , #1 Entity_Name [-] , ` becomes `Index [-], TimeStamp [s], #1 Entity_Name [-], `. The single space *after* each comma stays. This is not part of the logging-backend swap, despite arriving in the same range — upstream dropped spdlog in 2.50.0 and removed the space separately in 2.50.4 — so changing the backend does not give you this for free; it is its own edit. **Write the header the new way.** The gate group that reads the header's shape matches the column names with the spacing left free, so that group passes on either spelling; a separate scored check asserts the new spelling directly, and a tree that keeps the old one loses it. The header your tree produces also carries forward, and later work reads it in exactly the spelling above. The column names, the field count and the row shape are read everywhere and must not change.

Your tree is at v2.51.1 after this step. The build profile is unchanged: still offline, still without OpenSceneGraph, OSI, SUMO, PROJ, ImPlot or the unit-test targets, so `odrviewer` and `replayer` are still not built.

All five fixtures listed above are still present, and each still runs, loads or converts at v2.51.1.

One caution carried forward from the earlier steps: the road manager's position setters return a family of non-negative success codes rather than only zero, and have done since 2.21.2. Callers distinguish success from failure by the sign. Narrowing success back to exactly zero is a regression.

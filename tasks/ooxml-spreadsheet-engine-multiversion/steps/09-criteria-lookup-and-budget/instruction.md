# Milestone 09 of 10 — Criteria, lookup, shared formulas and the decompression budget

You are continuing `seedcalc`, a Go library for OOXML SpreadsheetML documents,
in `/app`. The workspace already holds everything the previous milestones left

## 1. Implement this milestone

This milestone takes the registered formula function set from 233 names to 329 — 96 names added, **none renamed and none removed**, in deliberate contrast with milestone 08. Four families arrive at once. The criteria family is completed by `COUNTIF`, `COUNTIFS`, `AVERAGEIF`, `MAXIFS` and `MINIFS`, and the criteria parser behind them and the inherited `SUMIF` is rebuilt. The lookup and reference family gains `INDEX`, `MATCH`, `XLOOKUP`, `ADDRESS`, `TRANSPOSE`, `SHEETS`, `ISFORMULA`, `ISLOGICAL` and `ISREF`. The date and time family gains `DATEVALUE`, `DAY`, `DAYS`, `MONTH`, `YEAR`, `MINUTE`, `TIME`, `WEEKDAY`, `ISOWEEKNUM` and `YEARFRAC`, all working in the serial-number representation the workbook stores dates in. And the statistical, engineering and financial families gain twenty-five, ten and thirty-three names respectively — the distribution, rank and percentile functions; `BESSELK`, `BESSELY`, the `ERF`/`GESTEP` group, `TEXTJOIN` and `VALUE`; and the bond, security, annuity and depreciation functions. Alongside the library, the comparison and concatenation operators learn to handle text operands, which until now made the whole formula invalid, and the conditional functions `IFNA`, `IFS`, `SWITCH` and `XOR` arrive.

Outside the function library there are six changes. Shared formulas become functional: the `FormulaOpts` tail milestone 04 added to `SetCellFormula` stopped at writing `@t` and `@ref` onto the master cell, and now it populates the whole declared range with a library-assigned `si`, so a formula written once in a master cell is read back from every cell of the range translated to that cell's position. The library's diagnostics become thirty-six exported `Err*` sentinel values a caller can branch on with `errors.Is`, phrased in terms of exported limit constants; milestone 08 exported none of them, so every entry point that reported a failure as an assembled string must now return the right sentinel. The `Options` struct grows three fields — `RawCellValue` and the two decompression budgets `UnzipSizeLimit` and `UnzipXMLSizeLimit` — and every reading entry point (`GetCellValue`, `GetRows`, `GetCols`, `Rows.Columns`, `Cols.Rows`) takes the same variadic tail. And a set of accessors fills gaps: `GetCellType` with its `CellType` enumeration, `GetAppProps`/`SetAppProps` over a new `AppProperties` struct, `SetRowStyle`, a `RowOpts` tail on `StreamWriter.SetRow`, the iterator position accessors `CurrentRow`/`TotalRows`/`CurrentCol`/`TotalCols`, `Rows.Close` and `File.Close`, and `ReadZipReader` moved from a package-level function to a method on `*File`. Four exported map fields on `File` — `Drawings`, `Sheet`, `Relationships` and `Pkg` — become `sync.Map`s where milestone 08 held plain Go maps, so a lookup is `Load` and a traversal is `Range`; `Pkg` is also a rename, milestone 08 having called the parts map `XLSX map[string][]byte`, and that field no longer exists (§4.09.14). And `PivotTableField` gains two layout flags, `Compact` and `Outline`, written through to `pivotField` as `@compact` and `@outline` and always emitted spelt out, so a zero-valued axis field serialises with `compact="false" outline="false"` rather than with the attributes omitted (§4.09.16).

Several of the new functions depart from their OpenFormula definitions and are required exactly as the Oracle exhibits them. A text criterion is compiled as a Go regular expression and matched **without anchors**, so `=COUNTIF(B1:B4,"ap")` counts every value containing `ap`; the wildcards are translated into that expression, so `"*a"` matches anywhere rather than at the end. `ADDRESS` range-checks its row argument and then discards it, using the column number for both halves, so `=ADDRESS(2,3)` is `$C$3`. A mixed comparison never coerces: the numeric side is simply the smaller one, so `=1<"a"` is `TRUE`. A cell reference is classified as numeric before its contents are read, so a text-valued cell takes the number side of a mixed comparison. `DATEVALUE` discards any time of day in its argument and refuses a serial number as input. `VALUE` understands a trailing per-cent sign and exponent notation but does not strip currency symbols or thousands separators. Only `AMORDEGRC`, `AMORLINC`, `XIRR` and `XNPV` force text dates through `DATEVALUE`; every other financial function accepts serial numbers. `SetAppProps` replaces rather than merges, so a zero field clears whatever the part held. And `GetCellType` reports `CellTypeUnset` for a numeric cell, because a number carries no type attribute at all.

Nine inherited contracts move here, and all nine are in scope. A numeric formula result is re-rendered to **fifteen significant digits**, but only when its shortest exact rendering needs **more than fifteen digits after the decimal point**; a result that already fits in fifteen decimal places is handed back unrounded, even at sixteen or seventeen significant digits. So `=SQRT(2)` is `1.4142135623731` and `=10/3` is `3.33333333333333`, while `=PI()` stays `3.141592653589793` and `=100/7` stays `14.285714285714286`. Because the trigger counts places and the rounding counts digits, a result below one gains a place (`=EFFECT(0.05,4)` is `0.0509453369140624`), and trailing zeros are dropped so an exact result sheds its accumulated binary tail (`=KURT` on a linear sample is `-1.2`, not `-1.199999999999999`). Reading a *stored* numeric cell re-renders at a lower threshold — more than ten decimal places — so the two call paths disagree; §4.09.17 has the whole rule and the counterexamples. The coordinate helpers accept an absolute reference, so `CellNameToCoordinates("$A$1")` is `(1, 1, nil)` where milestone 08 rejected it; the other malformed forms are still rejected. `GetSheetIndex` folds case, so `"sheet1"` resolves to `Sheet1`. `DataValidation.SetRange` takes two empty interfaces instead of two `int`s and dispatches on each end independently, so a bound may be a whole number, a decimal or formula text. `SHEET` takes an optional sheet name or reference where milestone 08 was nullary and rejected any argument. `RATE` is registered, reversing milestone 08's `not support RATE function`. A shared formula populates its whole range and translates on read, where milestone 04 wrote the two attributes onto the master cell alone and handed the untranslated text back from every other cell; the `ref` is now validated, and a `dataTable` type is dropped rather than written through. And every written part's XML declaration loses `standalone="yes"`, becoming the Go standard library's `xml.Header`; the exported `XMLHeader` constant is removed with it, while `XMLHeaderByte` remains. Every other inherited contract from milestones 01–08 remains in scope, including the milestone-08 quirks that did *not* move: the five dotted ceiling and floor renames, `VLOOKUP`'s miss being a Go error, `CONCAT` refusing a range, `FIXED` having no two-decimal default, `T` returning a non-text argument's string form, `HARMEAN` reading scalars only, `OCT2DEC` accepting the digit 8, `OR` assigning over numeric arguments, `TRIM` not collapsing interior whitespace, `ISNONTEXT` answering "is this operand empty", `DATE` rendering a formatted instant rather than a serial, and `StreamWriter.Flush` leaving the in-memory worksheet unpopulated.
